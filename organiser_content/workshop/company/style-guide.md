# NordicBike AB — Public Communication Style Guide

This guide defines how NordicBike AB communicates externally: on the website, in customer support, in product documentation, and in any written material a customer or partner may read. It applies to everyone who writes on behalf of the company.

---

## Voice

NordicBike AB communicates like an engineer who happens to be good with words. We explain things because we believe customers deserve to understand what they are buying and what we owe them. We do not perform enthusiasm and we do not manufacture urgency.

**Practical.** We describe what the product does and what the company will do. We do not describe feelings the customer should have.

**Precise.** We use exact figures. We do not say "long battery life" when we mean "up to 80 km". We do not say "quickly" when we can say "same business day" or "within 4 business hours". Vague reassurance is not a substitute for accurate information.

**Direct.** We lead with the answer. A customer asking whether their bike is under warranty should have the answer in the first sentence of the reply, not at the end of a paragraph explaining our process.

**Honest about limits.** We say what we do not cover as clearly as what we do. We say "we are unable to" rather than burying an exclusion in passive voice. We acknowledge uncertainty rather than projecting false confidence.

**Understated.** We do not use superlatives. We have never described a product as "revolutionary", "world-class", or "the best". Our bikes work well in the conditions they were designed for. We say that.

---

## Tone

Tone adjusts to context. Voice does not.

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Product descriptions | Informative, confident, brief |
| Customer support | Warm, direct, solution-oriented |
| Warranty and policy | Precise, neutral, complete |
| Error messages and rejections | Respectful, factual, next-step focused |
| Social and campaign copy | Human, dry, low-key |

**Support tone in practice.** In a support conversation, we acknowledge what the customer experienced before explaining what we will do about it. We do not over-apologise or repeat apologies. One acknowledgement is sufficient. We do not use phrases that imply the customer is wrong or naive.

## Language Rules

### Use plain words

We write in the language the customer used to contact us. If a customer writes in Swedish, we respond in Swedish. If they write in English, we respond in English.

We do not use internal terminology in external communication. Words like "eligibility", "claim window", "coverage term", and "section number" are internal. External equivalents:

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Warranty eligibility | Whether the repair is covered |
| Claim window / coverage term | The warranty period |
| Section 4(i) exclusion | Not covered because of water damage |
| Escalation | Passing your case to our senior support team |
| Serial batch | Production batch (or avoid entirely) |

### Tense and voice

Use the present tense for facts that are currently true. Use active voice.

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "The warranty period will have been calculated from..." | "Your warranty runs from the purchase date." |
| "Coverage may be provided in instances where..." | "We cover manufacturing defects for 24 months." |
| "The bike was found to have..." | "The bike has a manufacturing defect." |

### Numbers and dates

- Dates: `2025-03-10` (ISO format) in written documents; "10 March 2025" in customer-facing prose
- Currency: `34 900 SEK` with a space as thousands separator (Swedish convention)
- Percentages: `50%` not "fifty percent" in running text
- Time: `09:00–17:00 CET` (24-hour, with CET stated)
- Distances and ranges: spell out the unit — "up to 80 km", not "80km" or "80 kilometers"

### Product names

Always use the exact product name. Never abbreviate in external communication.

| Correct | Incorrect |
|---|---|
| Aurora X3 | Aurora, X3, the Aurora |
| Fjord Cargo | Fjord, Cargo bike |
| Vinter Pro | Vinter, VP |
| PowerPack 720 | PowerPack, PP720 |
| NordicBike AB | Nordic Bike, Nordicbike |

In Swedish copy, the company name remains NordicBike AB. Product names are not translated.

## What We Say and What We Do Not Say

### We do not promise outcomes we cannot guarantee

If a case is under investigation, we say so. We do not say "your bike will be repaired" before a diagnosis is complete. We do not say "we will have this resolved by Friday" unless we have confirmed that timeline internally.

### We do not speculate about root cause to customers

External communication states what the customer experienced and what we are doing about it. It does not include internal diagnostic reasoning, code references, system component names, or preliminary hypotheses. "We have identified a software issue affecting order status" is external-ready. "A bug in the status projection mapper is causing the Shipped status to be suppressed" is not.

### We do not discount or compensate in writing without authorisation

A support agent's written response does not offer or confirm any discount, refund, or compensation unless the agent has been explicitly authorised to do so. If a compensation outcome is being escalated, we tell the customer their case has been passed to our senior support team. We do not say "we are considering a refund" or "we may be able to offer something".

### We do not use urgency language

We do not say "act now", "limited time", "don't miss out", or equivalents. If an offer is time-limited, we state the end date factually: "This applies to purchases made before 31 August 2024."

### We do not blame the customer

If a warranty claim is not covered, we explain the reason factually without framing it as a mistake. "This damage is consistent with high-pressure washing near the battery compartment, which falls outside what the warranty covers" is correct. "Because you pressure-washed your bike incorrectly" is not.

## Customer Support Writing

### Structure

A support reply has three parts:

1. **Acknowledge** what the customer reported, in one sentence. Do not paraphrase in a way that implies they are wrong.
2. **State the outcome or next step** clearly. If coverage is confirmed, say so. If more information is needed, ask the one specific question that moves the case forward.
3. **Give the concrete next action** — what the customer needs to do, or what we will do, and by when.

### Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Thank you for reaching out to us today" | Generic filler |
| "I completely understand your frustration" | Overstated; one acknowledgement is enough |
| "Please do not hesitate to contact us" | Filler; if we want them to contact us, say for what |
| "As per our policy..." | Legalistic; explain the policy in plain language |
| "Unfortunately, we are unable to..." | Not wrong, but avoid leading with "unfortunately" — lead with the reason instead |
| "Rest assured..." | Hollow; say what we will actually do |

### Closings

A closing confirms the next step. It does not need a pleasantry.

**Good:** "We will send you a prepaid shipping label within one business day. Once the bike arrives at the Stockholm service centre, the repair typically takes 5–10 business days."

**Avoid:** "Thank you again for choosing NordicBike. We hope to hear from you soon and look forward to assisting you further."

## Swedish and English

NordicBike AB operates in Sweden and communicates primarily in Swedish with domestic customers. English is used for international customers and for internal technical documentation.

When writing in Swedish for customers:
- Use `du` (informal second person), not `ni` as a formal singular. The formal `ni` is not NordicBike's register.
- Product names remain in English as listed above.
- Policy terms may be translated into plain Swedish; do not use English legal phrasing in Swedish customer communications.

When writing in English for Swedish customers who have contacted us in English:
- Use British English spelling (organisation, colour, centre, programme).
- Do not translate Swedish place names. "Malmö", not "Malmo". "Hammarby Sjöstad", not "the Stockholm waterfront neighbourhood".

## What Good Looks Like

A customer reports that their Aurora X3 will not hold a charge after 18 months of ownership.

**Weak reply:**

> Thank you for contacting NordicBike! We're so sorry to hear you're experiencing issues with your Aurora X3. We completely understand how frustrating this must be. As per our warranty policy, your bike may be eligible for service. Please don't hesitate to send us your order details and we'll look into this for you!

**Strong reply:**

> Your Aurora X3 is within its 24-month warranty period. Based on what you've described, the charging issue sounds like it may be covered as a manufacturing defect.
>
> To confirm your eligibility and arrange the repair, we need your order number or the serial number from the frame (it begins AX3 or VTP). Once we have that, we will send you a prepaid shipping label for the nearest service centre — Stockholm, Gothenburg, or Malmö, whichever is most convenient.
>
> Repairs under warranty typically take 5–10 business days from the date we receive the bike.
